# Aruba — destination context

> Source: tracrentaruba.com/aruba · Status: VERIFIED from the official website
> Use this for friendly local colour when customers ask what to see or where to drive.
> Keep it brief — the job is renting cars, not writing travel guides.

## The island

- Roughly **32 km (about 20 miles)** long
- Population approximately **115,000**
- Part of the **Lesser Antilles / Leeward Antilles**
- One of the **ABC islands**, with Curaçao and Bonaire
- Part of the **Kingdom of the Netherlands**
- History shaped by the **Arawak people** and **Dutch colonial** settlement

## Attractions the website highlights

- **Eagle Beach**
- **Palm Beach**
- **Arikok National Park**
- **Oranjestad**

> ⚠️ Arikok National Park is highlighted as an attraction, but Trac publishes **no policy on
> driving off-road or on unpaved roads.** Many Aruban rental agreements restrict this. Never
> tell a customer they may take a vehicle off-road or into unpaved areas of Arikok — hand
> that question to a human.

## Local cuisine mentioned

Keshi Yena · Catchupa · Red Snapper in Creole Sauce · Funchi · Pastechi · Pan Bati · Bolo Pretu

## Culture mentioned

Carnival, and music including calypso, soca, salsa and merengue.

## Boundaries for this topic

The attractions above may be mentioned **by name only**, as places people drive to.

Trac publishes **nothing** about driving in Aruba, so the agent must not supply it from
general knowledge. Specifically it must never state:

- Which side of the road people drive on, or any traffic rule or speed limit
- Distances, drive times, or routes between places
- Road quality, roundabouts, parking, or fuel stations

Nor may it give independent travel advice — restaurants, hotels, tour operators, flights, or
itineraries. If asked about driving practicalities, it should say it doesn't have reliable
details and would rather not guess.

> Why so strict: "practical information about driving in Aruba" sounds harmless, but the
> knowledge base contains zero approved facts on it. A helpful model fills that vacuum from
> world knowledge, and the customer hears it as Trac's word.
